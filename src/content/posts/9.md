This is just a short hello. I am ~50 pages into The Correspondent, which has gained a lot of buzz recently. I knew nothing about it but liked the two birds on the cover. This book is, at least thus far, a collection of letters to and from a woman named Sybil. In a passage I recently read, Sybil defends the practice of writing letters as a way to leave a tangible, lasting mark on the Earth. I'd like to do that as well.

Most of the messages I write these days are in Slack (how sad is that!). Those messages will be there until I one day leave MLB, at which point if any future employee were to come across my message, they'll see my greyed out profile photo of Stomper the elephant in a suit. 

Second on that list is probably Facebook Messenger. Those messages will live as long as Facebook does, which is probably very long. Probably. But given how shitty the app is these days, and how awful it is to go back and look for old messages, those old messages likely won't have a lot of viewers in the future.

Third is SMS texting. Those messages are the opposite of enduring. I remember one day in college when Becca accidentially wiped all my text messages from my phone. Why is that even a feature? Strange.

Historically for me, I'm not one to go back and revisit old memories. I haven't cracked open any yearbooks recently, my journaling habit died before it ever got off the ground, and I even get a little embarassed looking at old things I used to do. But I think that might change sometime. Time moves very rapidly, and even if I can't stop that feeling, I can set some markers in the ground that say "hey, I was here at this point." Maybe one day, someone will read these and get something from them. I think that person will be me. Hopefully one day I'll move this to an even more physical medium. But for now, this is enough.

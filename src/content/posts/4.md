December has ended, Christmas is over, and 2024 is gone. Despite all the hand-wringing about holiday decorations showing up too early & the debate over when it's okay to start festive music, the whole season always ends before you know it. I made a point of it this year to really try and appreciate what I love so much about this season. Here are my thoughts, scattered and unfiltered and earnest.

Christmas has a location, and that place is Fremont. We often take for granted the natural beauty of our hometown, but it really is an ideal place for a California winter. The weather is usually quite mild enough to go on those neighborhood runs every other day. I adore every aspect of those runs: the awkward hand waves to the walkers coming from the other direction, the handful of kids shooting around on the old rim at Plomosa park, the uncles and aunties going on their ritual strolls. Watching over it all is our section of the Diabo range, a gorgeous backdrop to our city that we often take for granted. I took a second on my run to take in the beauty of the bottom of the hills that had just started to turn green while the tops were still covered in the morning clouds. As cookie-cutter suburbia as Fremont as, those mountains are uniquely Fremont in my mind.

Christmas is my house. Over the years, the furniture has been refreshed, the kitchen has been re-done, the rooms have switched places, but I know this house as well as anything on Earth. This will forever be home for me. We set up the Christmas tree in the living room with new lights this year, it's a beautiful sight to see when every other light in the house is turned off. One afternoon, I put on the Vince Guaraldi Trio Christmas album on vinyl, sat down on the red couch next to the tree, read for a bit, and took a very content and peaceful nap. It was one of the best naps of my career. 

Christmas is the rituals we do when everyone is in the house together. Mom makes chai in the afternoon and we all sit down and drink together while some sporting event plays on in the background. The annual jigsaw puzzle I bought lays unsolved in the dining room for days, inviting all of us bystanders to sit down for 5 minutes and try and put a couple pieces together. The beautiful granite of the breakfast island is largely covered up by the even more beautiful sight of freshly baked sweets for all of us to enjoy, an almost worrying amount of sugar waiting to be consumed (though most of my worries were placated by the excuse of "its December!"). 

But above all, Christmas is about the people. It's so wonderful to be under the same roof as my family. We go through the entire year without ever reuniting as one, but we always make it a priority to be together as the year turns new. The year is filled with different degrees of ups and downs for everyone, but everything is levelled out once we're at home for the holidays. Everyone eats together, everyone does the same activities, everyone goes to the same holiday events. All the conversations are of the hopeful and respectful type as opposed to the anxiety-inducing and worrysome questions that we struggle with during the rest of the year. This is our time to simply be together, without any real aim beyond that. Some might see that as aimless and boring at times, but I know we all appreciate the time to just exist in a safe and loving environment without any other worries. That's a luxury that many people out there do not consistently have access to, and it's good to enjoy every moment of it while it lasts.

All these things are constants over every Christmas I can remember, and I would never have it any other way. This is my Christmas, and though I know it will look a little bit more different every year as we get a little bit older every year, I hope it never stops feeling familiar.

For the last time this season, Merry Christmas!

What a beautiful city!

We're taking a rest back at the Airbnb before going out for our last dinner in Edinburgh. I'm drinking my tea while looking out to the north watching the trains go by. I'll wave at a couple on the off chance that some small child is mindlessly looking at the window and happens to catch me saying hello. Most of the rest are taking little naps, gathering energy for one more night of dinner and drinks here.

It's a gorgeous city. It's rather shocking how lovely every bit of our stay has been thus far. Our very first foray into the city was at a church's community cafe, where we had a very homely breakfast of simple fare: yogurt, scones, bread rolls, and americanos all around. With the posters showcasing community events, a space for kids to play, and a group of old friends chatting away in the back, you could feel the warmth radiating through the place. The workers shared the love with us as they served us our food, gave us a few pointers, and sent us on our way.

Arthur's Seat was our first stop after that and it served as a wonderful introduction to the rest of our stay. The hike was surprisingly arduous as the sun broke through the cloud cover, pouring the heat on our multiple layers of clothes until we were forced to remove the outer layers. Once we got to the top, we were greeted with a sweeping view of the entire city to the north: the city centre and old town directly in front of us, the sprawling residential areas expanding behind that, and the Port of Leith setting the north border in our field of view. To the south, the burned area of the hill from a fire a few months ago, and a collage of expansive suburban areas and smaller hills. We took our photos, took in the air, and descended.

The cite centre is beautiful and really something to behold. There's a massive ditch in the middle that serves as the train hub and public park, and towering over all of that is the Edinburgh castle. We were lucky enough to take a tour of the old building, which has a lot of history and an interesting life, but nothing really beats the view of it from the ground of the city streets. It's a gorgeous building that keeps watch over everything else in the city, a constant suggestion that we're somewhere elsewhere, somewhere more magical. I love it, and I wonder if the residents ever get tired of seeing it.

I know that it's hard to get the complete feel of a city just from a few days doing all the touristy bits, but this felt like maybe the city I could see myself living in most. It's got such a charming feel to it, with everything being either built hundreds of years ago, or restored to capture that same feeling. It's always a shock to remember just how new America is as a whole as comparison, even more so the west coast. Edinburgh is a city that has its history pulsing through its veins, and I wish I could spend even more time here.

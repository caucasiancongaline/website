High tea was an unexpected delight. I always wrote it off as Victorian England cosplay, something people do more for the camera than actual enjoyment. This experience really changed my mind on that.

We were greeted warmly in the hotel by staff and led to our room on the far end of the dining room. Our circular table was adjacent to the bay windows, giving us a view of the main road and the River Ness just beyond it. The server came around, she took our tea orders, and we settled in for a very enjoyable meal.

One thing that makes high tea feel so high class is the sheer number of dishes used. For myself, I had my teapot, milk carafe, teacup, plate for the tea cup, tea spoon, tea strainer, tea strainer holder, plate for the tea strainer, water glass, two plates, two knives, two forks, and a spoon. 16 items for each individual, with additional dishware coming for the food. This is a long road from my typical desire to use as few dishes as possible as to reduce the cleaning load on myself. Using anything more than 4 dishes feels like real luxury to me. I should invest in little small plates like the ones they had, they are an unexpected delight.

The food was awesome. Thee's so much attention to detail with these little dishes -- again, very different from the way I usually cook for myself. There is no way in hell I could ever make so many different dishes with so many different flavors all at once. To have them all on my plate at the same time is a wonderful luxury. All these flavors from around the world, all these techniques of cooking, all these ingredients sourced from across the globe, everything coinciding to one tower of little delectable bites. It's a special thing, an incredibly high-effort meal altogether, and it makes sense that this was only able to be enjoyed by royalty. Something like this would be unthinkable for working-class Brits, as despite the smallness of the stature of the food, the scope of it just too large for the masses. I felt thankful after finishing my meal that this was something I was able to do. 

After dinner, we headed back to our stay at a cottage in Muir of Ord. I believe that this was my first time in a small town / suburb within the UK, and it was a delight. Unlike in the states, their roads are still rather compact and pedestrian friendly, even for places that don't get a ton of foot traffic. The roads meandered out from the city centre / train station in a seemingly haphazard yet compact manner.

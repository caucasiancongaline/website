The sun was beaming down as the game rolled over to the top of the 9th, Oakland still up by a run. I had finally finished my disgusting space dust IPA that had performed its duties well in putting me in a mild stupor as I watched my team take the field for the last time ever. The kelly greens popped under the cloudless sky, the sun lit up the field for one last time.

The Coliseum is a beautiful place. Utilitarian in every sense of the word, it is a place that has worked to serve the people of the East Bay. It is not flashy, it is not aesthetic, it is not something that would ever be built today. It is a concrete heat magnet that has been neglected by the city and county for decades. But the people of the East Bay have loved it the same throughout everything, the good years and the bad. It has been a gathering place, a place to cheer, a place to forget about everything else for a bit and just take in a ballgame under the sun with the people you care about. That's all a stadium really needs to be, and the memories we make while doing so fill in the rest.

Mason Miller was cranking it up to 103 as the crowd continued buzzing, a bit more nervously now than before. We still clapped for every strike, made noise when the count went 0-2, but our minds were elsewhere. This was going to be it, and I spent more time taking mental snapshots of everything around me rather than laser-focusing on the game. 

I saw the mass of fans dressed in green, yellow, and white in front of the light grey concrete backdrop of the building. I heard the drums beating to my left, where they've always been, in the right field bleachers. I saw the flags waving a few rows ahead of me, the curisve 'Oakland' waving in the wind.

And eventually, I saw the batter slap a fastball to short, an easy putout at first, and that was that. The crowd roared, team rolled out of the dugout, and the speakers started playing Kool & the Gang for the last time ever. Mark Kotsay came out to address the crowd, the players saluted us, we clapped for them, a mutual show of appreciation for 60 years of memories that predated most of us and them.

Jeff, Auggie, and I stuck around for the better part of an hour. We baked in the sun taking in the expanse of the field for one last time. We traded stories of our memories made in this building, both the ones we were here for and the ones we remember seeing on the TV. We walked down to the front of the section, watching as the groundskeepers obliged every fan request to save a little bit of the dirt as a keepsake. Someone left flowers in a cupholder, bright gold to match the theme. Eventually we took our last looks, and headed towards the exits.

The walk back across the BART bridge still took forever, but we were happy to soak it in. We walked by the vendors selling those delicious hot dogs & the men hawking their off-brand merchandise while the chants of 'Lets go Oakland' sprouted up intermittently, one last cry while we still could. I snuck one last look at the cathedral before I ducked down the stairs, saying goodbye for the last time.

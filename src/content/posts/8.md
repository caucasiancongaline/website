Double play to end it. The A’s lose 10-0. This is the 3rd A’s game I’ve watched this weekend and they’ve scored a total of 3 runs. Yeah, that seems about right.

I let everyone do their jobs. The clock operator packs up, the scoreboard room starts cleaning up the wires, and the beat writers make dinner plans. I hang back, send “game over” updates in the slack channels, and loiter around a bit. I shake hands with the ABS techs — their day was pretty boring with just a couple of challenges. They shuffle out of the box and I go back to my computer, lazily completing the one thing I have left to work on for the day. I work slowly, given my tiredness and lack of plans. I have 8 hours until my flight. 

Soon enough the entire press box has cleared out and I’m by myself up here. “Closing Time” has finished playing over the speakers and the buzz of the fans has left the building. There are three main sounds I hear:

1. The grounds crew at work. There’s a guy who taps down the dirt with some kind of specialized tool, which is not something I fully grasp the importance of. There’s two guys operating a hose, watering every inch of the diamond. It seems like way too much water but it’s probably not, the Arizona sun is unforgiving. There are a few others who also have their own specialized tasks, and they all work in confident, practiced motions. There is no wasted movement, they all know exactly what needs to be done, as they’ve all completed this same maneuver dozens, hundreds of times. It’s hypnotic to watch.
2. The custodians cleaning the seats. Empty plastic cups are tossed into gigantic plastic trash bags. The custodians casually speak to each other while they’re doing it, but my Spanish isn’t good enough yet to pick up on what they’re saying. This is a job that could never seem enjoyable to me, but they don’t seem to be as cranky as I would be. One of them says something very funny that the others laugh at. I feel left out. I promise to myself that I’ll practice my Spanish later that night (it doesn’t happen).
3. The birds. I’ve kind of done a 180 on birds. I used to think they were annoying and unimpressive for most of my life up until recently. Now I increasingly appreciate them for what they are. How can there be thousands of types of these little guys, each one with their own coat, mannerisms, and behaviors??? That’s so many. My focus now is on their songs. There is one specific type of bird that is having a good ole time singing their hearts out collectively. I have no idea what kind of bird this is, what they look like, or if they were even in the stadium. All I know is that I could hear their sweet song playing the melody amongst the backdrop of the other ambient sounds.

The window at my seat is open, so all of these noises come to me unabated. The day is warm, but the sun has started its descent downwards. What this creates for me is a pleasant amber feeling of warmth, peaceful noise, and the visual of the beautiful diamond. It’s a beautiful cocktail of senses I try to take it all in as a memory. How lucky I am to be spending my Tuesday afternoon at this particular place in the world. I’ve got a million things to do but they all fade away at this time. All I can think about is the song of those birds. What a luxury.

I linger until I realize that the game ended nearly an hour and a half ago, and I make my exit out of the press box and downstairs to the concourse. Nobody’s really around anymore, just some workers in their karts driving things around to where they need to be. Every exit I walk by is locked up, and I realize that the only place left open is probably the staff gate in right field. I take the long way around the field level and finally walk out the park. I say goodbye to the security workers and they wish me well.

A side note on the people of Arizona — they are the loveliest people you’ll ever meet. Every interaction I had this week was warm and welcoming. The sun has melted everyone enough to take things slowly and take care with their conversations. There’s no rush here. It’s easy to forget what it’s like to have a quick conversation for the sake of having a conversation. I appreciate the reminder.

I reach my car and I got ice cream on my mind. I find a place a short drive away and route to Downtown Mesa. I pick up on an episode of Cumtown as I drive. I know it’s a horrible show but I want to keep the happy vibes going. Nick says something crazy that I am not going to repeat here but I audibly giggle at. How does he do it.

I park my car in a two-hour slot and walk towards the shop. This part of the city feels different from everything else, as they have clearly tried to make this a walkable downtown with a bunch of small and cute stores around and people sitting outside for their meals. I pass by a lovely wine bar that looks like a place I could throw back three glasses of a chardonnay at. Maybe if I wasn’t driving. I head towards a tiny ice cream shop operating out of a window down a little alley. I get two scoops: blueberry cheesecake and a bourbon something or other. The bourbon one is instantly forgettable, and I eat around it. The blueberry cheesecake flavor is wonderful. Cheesecake does not get enough love in the modern day, it’s always so delightful. I sit down outside and eat my ice cream as we enter our last hour of sunlight. Two elderly British ladies sit down a few benches away. I wonder what they’re here for. Certainly not baseball. Maybe just ice cream.

I wander around the block. The weather is perfect right now: the sun is unimpeded as it hits my face, but it doesn’t have the strength of a 1:00 sun. It’s warm and comforting, and it lights up the world around me in a muted yellow hue. Golden hour, I guess. Looks great. I think about the blizzard back in New York. What a blessing it is to be out here at this time. I pass a brewery serving disgusting hoppy IPAs with a group of friends eating pizza on the patio, an old-school burger joint serving ice-cold Modelos, and a distillery currently offering 20% off all cocktails for happy hour. They are all calling my name and I must decline each one, as I need to operate a large automobile within the hour. I curse the world of car-centric infrastructure for requiring me to plan out my drinks well ahead of time, and I also curse the patrons of that brewery, burger joint, and distillery for being smarter than me. It’ll have to wait. I don’t have a ton of time left, so I sit down on an uncomfortable bench and just sit for about 15 minutes. I’m taking in the last of the sun’s rays before they’re gone, before I go back to the east coast, before I don’t feel this comfortable outside again until June. What a feeling it is just to be outside enjoying the way the air feels. While most of the US is freezing away, or at least under a cloud, I get to be in one of the few pockets with absolutely perfect conditions, enjoying a perfect day. Rewinding to maybe five years ago, the odds of me feeling this atmosphere was very low. I could be in Seattle right now. Maybe back in San Francisco, or somewhere in its suburbs. This isn’t to say I wouldn’t be happy or comfortable in those situations — I usually am no matter where I am. But I wouldn’t be feeling this warmth at this moment. That’s saved for this Jonathan. I cherish that gift for a few more minutes, watching those unidentifiable birds dance around against a bright pink backdrop. I walk across the street, get in my car, and head towards the airport.
